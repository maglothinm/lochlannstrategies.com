Five perspectives that inform the advice.

Across government and industry, the recurring work was the same: understand the customer, shape a credible solution, assemble the team, win the work, and deliver what was promised.

Government customer

Requirements & acquisition

In the U.S. Air Force, Michael led requirements and Pentagon liaison work for force-protection modernization. His acquisition work also included RFPs, statements of work, site requirements, systems engineering, and design reviews.

02

Founder & growth leader

Business development & market strategy

In industry, he built businesses and led business development across U.S. defense, other federal, and international markets, translating customer priorities into growth strategies, capture plans, and routes to market with primes and partners.

03

Capture leader

Pursuit & partner governance

He qualified opportunities, tracked emerging requirements, built partner teams and formal teaming agreements, and took pursuits through competitive strategy, bid investment, pricing, risk, reviews, and executive approval.

Program integrator

His programs connected technology, people, data, sites, and operations—from radar and security systems to ports, command centers, data centers, nuclear facilities, civil works, communications, installation, testing, and acceptance.

Program leader

Michael led cross-functional teams through program startup, site readiness, implementation, recovery, testing, acceptance, and handoff, while coordinating with government customers and aligning partner, engineering, commercial, operations, and field teams across countries.
